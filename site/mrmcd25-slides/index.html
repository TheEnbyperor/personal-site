The before times

  • Created as the British Transport Commission under the Transport Act 1947
  • The (then private) railways had no money left after the War, so this was one of the few options left to 'Keep Britain Moving'
  • Corporatised at the British Railways Board under the Transport Act 1962
  • Owned and controlled absolutely everything
    Hotels in/around stations; road freight to/from stations; ferry services to France, Belgium, and the Netherlands; Railway Air Services Limited; British Transport Police; British Rail Property Board; staff savings bank; telecoms network

The FoIA (and FoISA) have exemptions

  • Sec. 21 - information already reasonably accessible
  • Secs. 30 and 31 – investigations and prejudice to law enforcement
  • Sec. 34 – parliamentary privilege
  • Secs. 35 and 36 – government policy and prejudice
    to the effective conduct of public affairs
  • Sec. 43 – trade secrets and prejudice to commercial interests
  • And many more!

Not to worry, we came armed...

...with a list of reasons why exemptions don't apply

For the full reasons, go read the submissions on WhatDoTheyKnow.

Sec. 38 Health and safety

Rail ticketing does not form a part of the health and safety of the railways.

Sec. 40 Personal information

These standards and public keys contain no personal information within them, even if individual tickets issued following them may contain such information.

Sec. 43 p. 1 Trade secrets

The Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) states that for this exception to apply "disclosure should also be liable to cause real (or significant) harm to the owner or be advantageous to any rival". The use of public-private key cryptography in the issuance of Aztec tickets ensures that even with disclosure of the specification and public verifying keys there would be no risk of a malicious party forging tickets. Additionally, as the provision of train services in the UK is in effect a government regulated monopoly over certain geographical areas it is unlikely that the second leg of this test applies.

Sec. 43 p.2 Prejudice to commercial interest

The ICO states that “it is not sufficient for you to simply argue that because information is commercially sensitive, its disclosure would, or would be likely to, prejudice commercial interests. You must be able to demonstrate a causal relationship between the disclosure of the information in question and the prejudice you envisage.”

I understand that some of the documents requested may contain or be subject to confidentiality clauses placed by the Rail Delivery Group. The ICO states that “having a confidentiality clause in place does not guarantee that information will not be disclosed under FOIA”. For the above-mentioned reasons I believe any confidentiality clause you have with the RDG or any other party on this matter is overridden by the public interest and commercial sensitivity tests.

So, how does Slovenian Freedom of Information work?

  • Similar to the UK (Act on Access to Public Information)
  • Even companies performing services for the public (including private bus operators etc.) are subjects to it (as far as their public duties are concerned)
  • Public registry of all subjects and legal basis for it (ajpes.si/RZIJZ)
  • Special provision on dynamic data and APIs (which is great for stuff like Transitious)

Registry entry for SŽ - Passenger Transport

What we discovered

  • They at least didn't use bit boundaries but full bytes

  • We could reverse the rest of the enums with enough different tickets

Slovenian FoI Exceptions

We have a lot less exceptions than the UK:

  1. Classified information
  2. Trade secrets
  3. Personal data
  4. Breaching statistical secrecy
  5. Breach of the confidentiality of tax proceedings or tax secrecy
  6. Obtained or compiled during a (current) criminal prosecution
  7. Obtained or compiled during a (current) administrative proceeding
  8. Obtained or compiled during a (current) judicial proceeding
  9. Documents still being written that could be misunderstood
  10. Protected information about cultural or natural values
  11. Internal functioning of the authority
  12. Information defined as protected by the InfoSec Authority
